Case Study February 2026

How a global research firm cut expert interview turnaround from weeks to hours.

99R
99Ravens Team
A global market research firm
01 The Brief
What the client needed — and why it couldn't wait.

The firm employed over 80 senior analysts whose institutional knowledge existed only in their heads. When a key consultant left, six months of client context walked out with them. Leadership needed a system to capture how their best people think — not just what they know.

The engagement had a hard constraint: no new software stack, no LLM training runs, no data science team. Whatever we built had to integrate with their existing workflow in under 90 days.

02 The Work
Extraction, structure, and deployment in three phases.

We ran structured extraction sessions with five analysts across twelve weeks — capturing not just answers, but the reasoning chains behind them. Each session was transcribed, tagged, and fed into a custom ontology reflecting how the firm categorized client problems.

The output was a retrieval layer that could surface relevant analyst reasoning given a new client brief — a second opinion from their own institutional memory, on demand.

03 The Evidence
Numbers that changed the conversation internally.

Within three months of deployment, onboarding time for new analysts dropped significantly. For the first time, client handoffs between consultants required no knowledge-transfer calls.

White Paper February 2026

The Codification Imperative: Why Expert Knowledge Walks Out the Door

An evidence-based framework for organizations that want to capture how their best people think — before they can't.

Fab Dolan · 42 pages · PDF
Abstract

Tacit knowledge — the accumulated judgment, heuristics, and pattern recognition that distinguishes expert practitioners from competent ones — is the most valuable and least protected asset in any knowledge-intensive organization. Unlike documented processes or explicit methodologies, it cannot be transferred through training manuals or onboarding sessions.

This paper introduces the Expertise Codification Framework, a structured methodology for capturing, structuring, and deploying tacit knowledge in AI-powered systems. Drawing on three years of deployment evidence across market research, consulting, and financial services organizations, we examine the conditions under which codification succeeds or fails — and what separates a working expertise system from an expensive archive.
